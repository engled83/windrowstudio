Stephen Ridley: Why the Best Moments Can’t Be Planned

Stephen Ridley has played piano around the world. Some of his favorite stories happened when the plan went out the window, from improvising a concert with an orchestra to meeting musical heroes in the most unexpected places.

With Dane and Stephen RidleySeptember 25, 202659 min 41 sec
Watch the interviewListen on Spotify

Meet Stephen

Stephen Ridley is a British pianist and performer who has taken his music from the streets of London to concerts around the world. He also founded Ridley Academy, an online piano school. He tells Dane how playing live keeps giving him stories he never could have planned.

What we talked about

  • How an audience in Uzbekistan surprised him during a seven-and-a-half-hour solo show.
  • Meeting people through music while traveling and what stood out to him in India.
  • Improvising a concert with a Georgian orchestra after just seven minutes of rehearsal.
  • How years of physical performances led to a doctor treating his fingers mid-show.
  • Introducing Sting at a private show and later playing music at his home.
  • The moment Robbie Williams, Stephen’s musical hero, turned his car around to speak to him.
  • Why more than 160 finished recordings are still waiting to be released.
  • A skiing trip where learning to turn left was not quite enough.

The shows that took on a life of their own

Stephen says he usually knows the first song he will play, then lets the audience shape what comes next. In Tashkent, Uzbekistan, a solo performance became a seven-and-a-half-hour experience with a crowd he had never expected. At Dubai Expo, he joined a Georgian orchestra after just seven minutes together and improvised the show as it happened.

For him, the best part of traveling to play is getting to meet people beyond the concert venue. He talks about the hospitality he found in India, the pride he saw in Tashkent, and how playing music lets him take part in a place instead of just passing through.

Sting, Robbie Williams, and a few unforgettable nights

At an intimate show in Italy, Stephen found out he was introducing Sting only when a note appeared on his piano. He ended up sitting nearby for Sting’s set and later visited his home for a music-filled anniversary party. Stephen also recalls telling Robbie Williams how much his music meant to him, then watching Robbie’s car turn around so he could come back and say thanks.

The songs no one has heard yet

Stephen loves the energy of a live audience, but recording and releasing a finished song can feel very different. He tells Dane he has more than 160 recordings that have not been released, even though he listens to them himself. They also talk about why he is drawn to spontaneous performances and what might finally get him to put more of that music out into the world.
