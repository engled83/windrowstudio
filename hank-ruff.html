Hank Ruff: Modern Country With Classic Roots

Southern Indiana country artist Hank Ruff talks bluegrass beginnings, songwriting, touring with the Hellbenders, and building a sound that keeps classic country in the mix.

About Hank Ruff

Hank Ruff is a country artist from southern Indiana. In this conversation, he traces his path from playing mandolin around his dad's band to writing and performing original music across the Midwest and beyond. His sound draws on bluegrass, classic country, and the country music of the 1990s, while leaving room for contemporary production and songwriting.

Classic country, not a costume

Ruff says his earliest influences were bluegrass and older country records, then the late 1980s and 1990s country he connected with as a teenager. He does not describe his approach as trying to recreate an earlier era. Instead, he aims to keep the steel guitar, fiddle, direct songwriting, and other traditional details that make a country record sound like country, while allowing newer elements to show up naturally.

That perspective runs through the episode: a respect for artists such as Buck Owens, Merle Haggard, Brad Paisley, and Jon Pardi, but a focus on making music that feels honest to him now.

Song stories from the episode

“From Here”

Ruff explains that the hook for “From Here” arrived after months of working on the song. The idea connected to the transition out of college and a moment of hearing “Jack and Diane” after an Indiana University championship game.

“Whiskey Always Wins”

“Whiskey Always Wins” was an outside cut, meaning Ruff did not write it. He says he chose it because it felt personal and believable enough that he could have written it himself, which is his standard for recording a song from another writer.

Hellbender

Ruff included his first original song, “The Prettiest Angel,” as the final track on his Hellbender EP. He originally wrote it in middle school and performed it at a choir concert.
