Kendall Eugene: Starting Over and Writing From Real Life

Texas-rooted country artist Kendall Eugene talks rebuilding after 2023, landing on The Voice, growing his career while working a day job, and turning lived experience into songs that connect.

About Kendall Eugene

Kendall Eugene is a country artist originally from Texas and currently based in Manhattan, Kansas. In this conversation, he describes his sound as country shaped by southern rock and blues, with an approach that aims to feel raw, heavy, and personal.

He started on drums as a kid in church before moving into guitar, songwriting, and fronting bands. The episode follows the long road that brought him back to music as a solo artist, including a hard reset in 2023 and the momentum he has built since.

What we talked about

  • Growing up in Texas and finding a country sound with southern rock and blues underneath it
  • Starting on drums in church and eventually moving into songwriting and guitar
  • Rebuilding after losing his job, marriage, and home in 2023
  • Getting discovered for The Voice through an Instagram cover
  • Becoming the first contestant in the show’s history to receive a coach’s replay
  • Building a band and booking three shows from one four-song open-mic set
  • Using Nashville writers rounds to make connections and test songs
  • Balancing music, family, and a full-time sales job

Starting over without waiting for perfect conditions

Kendall is direct about the fact that his path back into music did not begin with a clean plan. After his life fell apart in 2023, he spent time learning songs, posting covers, and figuring out what kind of artist he actually was. A producer from The Voice found one of those Instagram posts and reached out.

That opportunity did not solve everything for him, but it gave him proof that he could keep moving. He came out of the show focused on doing the unglamorous work: contacting venues, playing rooms, building relationships, and giving people a reason to take a chance on his live show.

Song stories from the episode

“Already Gone”

“Already Gone” began with a single line that stayed with Kendall for two years: a couple sharing a bed but no longer sharing a heart. He used that hook to write a bluesy song about a marriage that has already ended emotionally. A live reaction later convinced him it was a song worth recording.

“Dust Bowl Blues”

“Dust Bowl Blues” draws from his family’s history in West Texas during the Dust Bowl. The song grew out of a hard day and the thought that if his ancestors had survived that, he could make it through his own struggle. It is not literally his story, but it is part of the history he carries.

“Prodigal”

Kendall says “Prodigal,” which was available at the time of the conversation, was inspired by the biblical prodigal son and by his own life. It reflects the same rule behind much of his writing: make the song personal enough to be true, but open enough for somebody else to recognize themselves in it.
