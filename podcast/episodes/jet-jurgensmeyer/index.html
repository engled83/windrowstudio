Jet Jurgensmeyer: From Empty Bars to Bigger Stages

From playing for four people in a Nashville bar to opening for Jake Owen, LOCASH, and Lakeview, Jet talks about the work between those moments, the songs he’s making, and the life that happens around it all.

With Dane and Jet JurgensmeyerOctober 23, 202637 min 35 sec
Watch the interviewListen to the episode

Meet Jet

Jet Jurgensmeyer is a country music artist, songwriter, and actor. He joins Dane to talk about growing as an independent artist, life on the road with his band, and balancing music and acting with his senior year at Belmont University.

What we talked about

  • Playing nearly empty Nashville bars and opening for Jake Owen, LOCASH, and Lakeview.
  • Life on the road with his band and performing at the University of Kentucky.
  • The story behind “Sane to My Crazy” and being involved in songwriting and production.
  • Writing with Nashville collaborators, working with session musicians, and “Drink Alone.”
  • Balancing college, acting, voiceover work, and a growing music career.
  • Smaller shows, connecting with an audience, and hearing people sing his lyrics back to him.
  • Finding inspiration, recharging, and looking ahead to life after graduation.

From empty rooms to bigger stages

Jet looks back on playing for four people in a Nashville bar and compares those early performances with opening for Jake Owen, LOCASH, and Lakeview. He talks about traveling with his band, playing at the University of Kentucky, and the experiences that keep him excited to get onstage.

They also get into the appeal of smaller, more intimate shows and what it feels like to hear someone sing his own lyrics back to him. The conversation makes room for both the bigger opportunities and the small moments that make the work worthwhile.

“Sane to My Crazy” and the work behind the songs

Jet shares the story behind “Sane to My Crazy” and why he wants to take part in every step, from writing a song to recording and producing it. He and Dane talk about his Nashville collaborators, working with session musicians, and the process behind songs including “Drink Alone.”

They also discuss where song ideas come from, the challenges of writing, and the difference between drawing from personal experience and telling a story. Jet talks about finding inspiration and taking time to recharge.

College, acting, and what comes next

Touring and releasing music are only part of Jet’s schedule. He is also in his senior year at Belmont University and has a career in acting and voiceover work. He talks about balancing those commitments and making room for college life while pursuing music.

To close, Jet and Dane look ahead to graduation and the next stage of his career. It is a conversation about building momentum while continuing to write, record, and play the shows that get him there.
