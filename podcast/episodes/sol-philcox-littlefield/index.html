Sol Philcox-Littlefield: Inside Nashville’s Recording Sessions

Who actually gets the call to play on a record? Sol joins Dane to talk about the friendships, decisions, and everyday work behind Nashville recording sessions, from independent projects to major-label albums.

With Dane and Sol Philcox-LittlefieldOctober 30, 202652 min 8 sec
Watch the interviewListen to the episode

Meet Sol

Sol Philcox-Littlefield is a session guitarist and producer whose work includes records with Luke Combs and Aaron Lewis. He joins Dane for a conversation about the people behind the music, how recording sessions come together, and the relationships that turn a day in the studio into years of working together.

What we talked about

  • How session musicians get hired and who puts the band together.
  • Splitting his time between playing guitar on records and producing them.
  • Why being good to work with matters and how musicians look after each other.
  • Producing Aaron Lewis’s album and recording at RCA Studio A.
  • Working on Luke Combs’s early music before anyone knew how big it would become.
  • Why a strong connection in the studio does not always lead to another record together.
  • Giving independent projects the same care as major-label records.

How the session actually comes together

Dane asks whether musicians are chosen through managers, agents, and formal meetings. Sol describes a much more personal process: producers calling players they trust, session leaders putting a band together, and friends recommending one another for the job.

The question is often whether someone is enjoyable to work with. Sol talks about the friendships built over years of sessions, the etiquette around scheduling, and the responsibility musicians feel to check on each other when somebody is having a difficult day.

Two days at RCA Studio A

When Aaron Lewis’s team needed an album finished on a deadline, they called someone they already knew and trusted. Sol explains how that long relationship led to the project and why he wanted to record the basic tracks together in one focused stretch.

He booked RCA Studio A, brought in musicians he loved working with, and recorded thirteen or fourteen songs in two days. He shares how the songs were chosen, why some familiar live material belonged on the record, and when it makes sense to let a great band’s performance stand on its own.

You never know where one project might lead

Sol remembers recording Luke Combs’s early music without knowing what it would become. The path into those sessions came through independent projects, friends, and friends of friends. What seemed like one small job could become the start of a much longer creative relationship.

That is why he wants to care just as much about a low-budget independent project as a major record. He and Dane also talk about connecting with someone in a studio and never working together again, and why that does not necessarily mean the session went badly.
