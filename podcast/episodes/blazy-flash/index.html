Blazy Flash of Beyond Frequencies: Starting Over at 40 & Building a Band

After a seven-year break from performing, Blazy Flash returned to music at 40 and built Beyond Frequencies from the ground up. She joins Dane to talk about second chances, finding the right musicians, and the work behind an independent rock band.

With Dane and Blazy FlashNovember 6, 202643 min
Watch the interviewListen to the episode

Meet Blazy

Blazy Flash is the vocalist and songwriter behind Swiss rock band Beyond Frequencies. She is also a leadership trainer and keynote speaker. She joins Dane to talk about the connection between those worlds, the stage persona that helped an introvert find her confidence, and what it takes to build a music career the second time around.

What we talked about

  • Being told by a record label that a breakthrough was unlikely after 30.
  • Returning to music at 40 after seven years away from performing.
  • Creating the Blazy Flash alter ego and overcoming stage fright.
  • Building Beyond Frequencies, finding musicians, and refining the audition process.
  • Turning piano demos into rock songs and creating a rawer live show.
  • Touring Europe, choosing set lists, and building an independent artist’s support network.
  • The meaning behind “1000 Suns” and funding a fourth studio album.

Starting over at 40

When her previous band ended, Blazy was exhausted by the work and uncertainty of a music career. A record label’s comment that she was probably too old to break through added to that experience, but it was not the only reason she stepped away. For seven years she stopped performing, even though she never stopped writing songs.

A guest appearance at a tribute concert helped bring her back to the stage. She talks about returning at 40 with more experience, a different perspective on criticism, and the confidence to try again. Her Blazy Flash persona became a way to step into the spotlight without losing who she is.

Building Beyond Frequencies

Blazy initially spent nearly a year looking for musicians who could help turn her piano demos into a new project. When that did not come together, she began working with two producers to make the first album. Having finished music to share made it easier to find the players who would bring the songs to the stage.

She explains why the live show is deliberately rawer than the recordings, how the band and sound engineer choose a set list, and why audience reactions can tell a different story than streaming numbers. She also describes an audition process that now includes performance videos and rehearsals before someone joins the band.

New music and the business behind it

Being independent still means working with other people. Blazy talks about building a network of producers, booking contacts, agencies, and distributors, and finding the live opportunities that can help the band grow. She also looks back on touring eight European countries with Semblant.

To close, Blazy shares the story behind “1000 Suns,” a song shaped by difficult periods and the hope of helping others see their own worth. At the time of recording, the band was working toward a fourth album in 2027, with an April release dependent on raising the money to record it. It is a candid look at the creative and financial work that happens before new music reaches a listener.
